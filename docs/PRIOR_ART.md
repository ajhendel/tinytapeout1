# PRIOR ART — enumeration checklist

Rule: novelty is verified by enumeration, never by a proxy. No claim ships until its row is CLOSED with the searches run, the papers read, and the residual claim written. "First X" is banned while the row is OPEN.

Status legend: OPEN = not yet enumerated. PARTIAL = known items listed, systematic sweep not done. CLOSED = swept, residual claim recorded.

Sweep of record: 2026-08-26. Tiny Tapeout index enumerated in full (27 shuttles, 4,572 projects, index snapshot dated 2026-07-04). Evidence file docs/sweeps/tt_index_sweep_2026-08-26.txt, regenerated by `python3 tools/tt_index_sweep.py --out <dir>`. Literature queries logged in the Query log section at the bottom.

## The one sentence that covers the chip as a whole

Rows 1, 2, 8 and 9 each license a residual for their own topic. A reader who
wants one sentence about the whole vehicle gets this one, and only this one:

> We found no prior open-silicon platform combining hardware-in-loop search over
> standard-cell drive variants, explicit node loading and per-site sabotage, with
> public model-to-measurement comparison.

It says what we looked for and did not find. It does not say the thing does not
exist. The method and its date go beside it every time: full enumeration of the
Tiny Tapeout index on 2026-08-26, 27 shuttles, 4,572 projects, plus the
literature queries logged at the bottom of this file, of which step 3 of the
sweep protocol was NOT run.

## Licensed and unlicensed language

Added 2026-08-27 after a design review caught several claims in conversation
that were stronger than this file supports. The file was right and the speech
was not, which is the failure mode the file exists to prevent, so the specific
wordings are recorded here rather than left as a resolution to be careful.

**Not licensed, and each one was actually said:**

- "Nobody has systematically checked the models at scale." Not established. The
  enumeration covers Tiny Tapeout, not the literature.
- "Nobody has searched over electrical realization on a measured die." Same
  problem. Row 2's residual is the licensed form and it is narrower.
- "Zero of 4,572 Tiny Tapeout designs do this, therefore it is novel." The
  premise is true and the inference is not. It is ecosystem evidence: it says
  what has been BUILT on this shuttle programme, not what has been PUBLISHED.
- "The genome picks the transistor width." It does not. It selects among
  prefabricated library cells whose internal sizing differs. Nothing on this
  chip resizes a transistor. The correct phrasing is "selects the drive variant
  of the standard cell that drives the node".
- Describing the fabric's feedback edge as a weaker or smaller oscillator Ising
  machine. There is no controllable coupling, no phase readout and no locking
  guarantee. See docs/MEASUREMENT_PROTOCOL.md.
- Describing the calibration strip as a thermometer. It is a joint process,
  voltage, temperature and activity covariate.
- Any figure for configurations per second stated as measured. See
  docs/THROUGHPUT.md; the only number measured so far is a simulation rate.

**Licensed:** the residual column in the table below, the sentence above, and
nothing else.

## Row notes

### Row 1 — intrinsic evolvable hardware. CLOSED.
Closest three works.
1. Thompson 1996, "An evolved circuit, intrinsic in silicon, entwined with physics", Xilinx XC6200 tone discriminator. The founding result and the reason the framing exists.
2. Stoica et al. (JPL), field programmable transistor array (FPTA-1, FPTA-2). A custom evolvable ASIC, transistor-level reconfigurable, with a genetic algorithm on a DSP, demonstrating functional recovery from faults and from extreme-temperature degradation. This is the work that makes "first evolvable ASIC" unavailable to us, permanently.
3. Loyd et al. 2025, "Bitstream Evolution: an Open-Source FPGA Intrinsic Evolvable Hardware Toolkit", IEEE Access, DOI 10.1109/ACCESS.2025.3631393, repo github.com/evolvablehardware/BitstreamEvolution, targeting the Lattice iCE40 through the IceStorm-reverse-engineered bitstream. Preceded by Whitley, Yoder and Carpenter, "Resurrecting FPGA Intrinsic Analog Evolvable Hardware", ALIFE 2021, which evolved amplitude maximization and pulse oscillation on iCE40 and tested temperature robustness and across-chip translation.

Enumeration result. The keyword class EVOLVE (evolv, genetic algorithm, evolutionary, darwin, hill climb, fitness function) matched **zero of 4,572 Tiny Tapeout projects across all 27 shuttles**. No evolvable-hardware substrate has been taped out on Tiny Tapeout on any PDK.

What this licenses. The residual sentence in the table. Note what it does not license. It does not license "first evolvable ASIC" (FPTA), "first modern intrinsic evolution" (Bitstream Evolution, ALIFE 2021), or any claim about evolution finding circuits humans would not (Thompson, settled in 1996).

### Row 2 — evolving the electrical realization. CLOSED, and this is the strongest row.
Closest three works.
1. Stoica FPTA. Transistor-level reconfigurable, so it moves *geometry*, which is a superset of what we do, but it is a custom analog array and its published targets are analog function synthesis and fault recovery, not the electrical realization of a fixed digital truth table measured against a model.
2. Automated gate sizing and transistor sizing under evolutionary search (for example logical-effort-plus-EA sizing with SPICE in the loop). The loop closes on a *simulator*, never on a measured die, which is exactly the gap we intend to instrument.
3. Post-silicon tunable buffers and clock tuning (EffiTest and the design-phase buffer allocation literature). Configurable delay in shipped silicon exists and is a standard technique, but it is tuned to meet a timing target, not searched, and it does not select cell variant, loading, or inserted faults.
4. Tiny Tapeout precedent, htfab's "MicroTapeout (of sky130 cells)" on tt03, 395 sky130_fd_sc_hd cells with a multiplexer exposing any of them to the pins, including every drive variant (buf_1/2/4/6/8/12/16, inv_1/2/4/6/8/12/16, nand2_1/2/4/8, and so on). This is the closest existing artifact to our block B site and it must be cited. It is a static observation bank, not a searched fabric, and it feeds the same six inputs to every cell in parallel, which is precisely the "parallel variants all switch together" confound recorded in PLAN.md section 2.

Enumeration result. The DRIVE keyword class matched 3 of 4,572 Tiny Tapeout projects, none of them a configurable-realization fabric. Adding MicroTapeout by hand gives 4. No Tiny Tapeout project searches over electrical realization.

Warning attached to this row. The strength of the row comes from the *search-against-the-die* qualifier, not from the fabric. Drop that qualifier and the row collapses into MicroTapeout plus the sizing literature.

### Row 3 — oscillator Ising machines. CLOSED, and it cost us the flagship framing.
Closest three works.
1. Wang and Roychowdhury, oscillator-based Ising machines, and the large CMOS coupled-oscillator literature 2019 onward. The concept, the phase-binarization mechanism, and sub-harmonic injection locking are all theirs.
2. A 48-node all-to-all coupled ring-oscillator Ising solver chip in 65 nm CMOS, and COBI. Silicon at a scale we cannot approach, with logic-based coupling, solving representative combinatorial optimization problems.
3. **Tiny Tapeout precedent on our own PDK, one shuttle ahead of us.** "WobblyBits, a probabilistic computing chip", Isaac W, shuttle TTSKY26a, 1x2 tiles, github.com/rats2012/WobblyBits. Six p-bits, an SPI-loadable symmetric 6x6 signed coupling matrix, neoTRNG entropy from three inverter rings, sampling an Ising/Boltzmann distribution on sky130. Its own repo describes it as "a **digital** probabilistic computing chip", and the update rule is a digital threshold comparison of a TRNG byte against a linear sigmoid approximation, so the dynamics are synchronous digital updates rather than continuous phase settling. That distinction is the only thing left between it and our block P, and it is a thin distinction that must be stated explicitly every time.
4. Also on sky130 Tiny Tapeout, "Tetrahedral Oscillator" (TTSKY26b, coupled CMOS inverter pairs with capacitive load tuning, custom Magic layout with PEX) and "Ring OSC Speed Test" (tt02, two rings of equal stage count, explicitly measuring whether they influence each other).

Consequence for PLAN.md. The candidate residual in the old row 3, "open-silicon OIM with search-configurable coupling, reproducible for about 500 euros", was written before this sweep and is now too strong. WobblyBits is open-silicon, on sky130, with a loadable coupling matrix, for the price of two tiles. PLAN.md has been corrected in the same commit as this file.

What is left, stated precisely. Continuous-time phase dynamics of physically coupled ring oscillators, with the coupling ladder itself searched in-loop against the die, compared on the same chip against a digitally-updated control. That is a real experiment. It is not a first.

### Row 4 — p-bits. PARTIAL, and the obvious claim is dead.
The old candidate residual, "open-PDK all-standard-cell p-bit patch with published noise characterization", is dead on arrival. WobblyBits is exactly that, on sky130, already fabricated. Camsari and Datta's p-bit work and the MTJ demonstrations own the concept.

What could still be ours is the *physically marginal element*, a bistable biased into its metastable region so that thermal noise, not a TRNG, sets the bit. That needs tier-3 custom cells and belongs to tapeout two. No p-bit sentence may be written for tapeout one. On tapeout one the p-bit mode is a measurement, not a claim, and any writeup cites WobblyBits.

Sweep evidence. PBIT class matched 11 of 4,572, of which the stochastic-computing entries are unrelated (stochastic *arithmetic*, bitstream multipliers), and WobblyBits is the only true p-bit array.

### Row 5 — analog and continuous-time SAT. PARTIAL.
Ercsey-Ravasz and Toroczkai 2011 mapping, the continuous-time MaxSAT solver in Nature Communications 2018, "Efficient Analog Circuits for Boolean Satisfiability", and SPICE-level modeling of continuous-time SAT solvers (arXiv 2412.14690). CMOS oscillator prototypes exist. Not claimed on tapeout one. Queries logged below. To CLOSE this row before any tapeout-two claim, the missing work is enumerating fabricated (not simulated) continuous-time SAT silicon.

### Row 6 — race logic. PARTIAL.
Madhavan, Sherwood and Strukov, ISCA 2014, and the 180 nm CMOS DNA sequence alignment engine at CICC 2017, so race logic is fabricated silicon. NIST continued the line. Not claimed on tapeout one. The only angle we would ever have is designed delay elements plus our own TDC resolving sub-gate-delay arrival order, which is a measurement capability, not a computing result.

### Row 7 — FPGA coupling side channels. PARTIAL.
Ramesh et al. long-wire crosstalk, Giechaskiel et al., shared-supply attacks. Existence on FPGA is settled and we never claim otherwise. Residual as in the table, conditional on block D surviving the area budget. Note block D is the smallest and first-dropped block in PLAN.md section 3, so this row may never need closing.

### Row 8 — sky130 silicon versus model. CLOSED. Block A claims nothing.
Enumeration found four direct precedents on Tiny Tapeout.
1. "SKY130 technology characterization", Andreas Madner, shuttle TTSKY25b, github.com/analogandi/ttsky-um-technology-characterization, described as "first attempt in characterizing the SKY130 technology and perform aging experiments". Its docs/info.md is still a template placeholder, so no results are published, but the intent is prior and it is on our exact PDK.
2. "MicroASIC VI", Mikhail Svarichevsky, tt02, "free-running oscillators to verify simulation vs reality plus TRNG". This is silicon-versus-simulation on sky130 Tiny Tapeout in 2022.
3. "Ring Oscillator PVT Sensor and TRNG", Santhosh Sivasubramani, TTSKY26a and a GF180 port on TTGF26a. Five SPI-configurable ring oscillators of 7/11/15/21/31 stages, 16-bit frequency counter, configurable prescaler, differential beat-frequency mode, explicitly for PVT characterization and cross-foundry comparison.
4. "IHP Gate Delay Characterizer (3-Flavor)", Catalin Lazar, TTIHP26a, ring oscillators built from Inv1, Inv2 and Inv4 drive variants with a 24-bit counter. This is drive-variant delay characterization by ring oscillator, on IHP rather than sky130.

Also relevant. tt04 "Multi stage path for delay measurements" documents, in its own project description, that the Tiny Tapeout synthesis flow refused a combinational ring-oscillator loop and collapsed cascaded inverters into buffers. That is a recorded precedent for the WP2 risk and is cited in docs/THROUGHPUT.md.

Consequence. Block A is calibration infrastructure that makes block B and block P defensible. It generates no novelty sentence. Any writeup of block A cites all four projects above.

### Row 9 — fault insertion and mutation on silicon. CLOSED.
Closest three works.
1. Classical DFT and fault-injection literature. Fault injectors on gate inputs and outputs with decoder-selected fault commands are decades old, as is scan-based fault insertion.
2. Real-time fault injection through on-chip debug infrastructure (Journal of Systems Architecture 2010 line of work), which injects into memory elements rather than into combinational realization.
3. The software mutation-testing literature and its explicit comparison to hardware fault injection, both being fault-simulation-based techniques.

Enumeration result. FAULT class matched 6 of 4,572 Tiny Tapeout projects. The closest is "Glitcher" (pakesson, on TTIHP0p4, TTIHP26a and TTSKY26a), which *generates* voltage or clock glitches for attacking an external target, and "BIST-8" (TTGF26a), a built-in self-test for an adder. Neither inserts per-gate faults into a configurable fabric.

What this licenses. Not fault injection, which is ancient. The residual is the transfer measurement, that is, how well a mutation kill set derived in simulation predicts the kill set measured on the die once the circuit is pushed to the timing and voltage edge where the two need not agree.

## Sweep protocol per row
1. Google Scholar and Semantic Scholar keyword sweeps, with every query logged in the Query log below.
2. Tiny Tapeout project index sweep across all shuttles, via `tools/tt_index_sweep.py`, evidence committed under docs/sweeps/.
3. FOSSi Foundation, ORConf and Latch-Up talk archives.
4. Cite the closest 3 works in the row even when the finding is CLOSED-favorable.
5. Write the residual claim in one sentence. That sentence is the only form that may appear in a paper.

Step 3 was not run in the 2026-08-26 sweep. It is outstanding for rows 1, 2, 3 and 8, and is recorded here rather than quietly skipped. It is unlikely to overturn rows 1 or 2 and could plausibly add a precedent to row 8, which claims nothing anyway.
